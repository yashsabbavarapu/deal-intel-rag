# Competitor Battlecard: Competitor X

Internal only. Never share verbatim with a customer or paste into a shared Slack
channel. Last updated 2026-09-02 by Competitive Intelligence.

## Competitor X Overview

Competitor X is a Series D company (raised $180M at a $1.4B valuation in March 2025),
roughly 900 employees, with an estimated $140M ARR growing about 35% year over year.
They win most often in mid-market marketing-led deals and in accounts where the buyer
is a single-department champion with a sub-$50K budget.

We encounter them in about 41% of our competitive Enterprise cycles. Our head-to-head
win rate is 58% overall, and 71% when Security or IT is on the buying committee.

## Competitor X Strengths

Do not pretend these are weak. Acknowledge and reframe.

Their onboarding is genuinely fast — a self-serve team can be live in under an hour,
versus our typical two-week guided implementation. Their UI wins design bake-offs; it
is cleaner and less dense than ours. They are aggressive on price in the first year,
routinely discounting 40% or more to land a logo, and their marketplace has about
300 integrations to our 140.

They also publish a free tier, which builds bottoms-up awareness that we do not match.

## Competitor X Weaknesses

Their SOC 2 Type II covers Security only — not Availability or Confidentiality — and
they do not sign a BAA at all, so they are disqualified from any HIPAA workload. SAML
SSO sits behind their top "Enterprise Plus" tier at roughly a 3x price step, and SCIM
is not offered.

Their audit log has no export API, which fails most financial services and healthcare
security reviews. They have no data residency options outside us-east-1. Support is
email-only below $100K ACV with a 48-hour first-response SLA, and they publish no
uptime SLA with financial credits. Their renewal pricing is the recurring complaint in
G2 reviews: year-two increases of 25 to 60% are common once the land discount expires.

## Displacement Tactics

Lead with the compliance gap, not with features. Ask the security stakeholder directly:
"Will your auditor accept a Security-only SOC 2 report, and what is your plan for the
audit log export requirement?" That question lands the deal in our column more often
than any feature comparison.

Second, force total-cost-of-ownership over three years, not year one. Build the TCO
model with their year-two uplift included; our three-year TCO comes in 18 to 22% lower
in a typical 400-seat deal even when we lose year one on sticker price.

Third, when the champion is a single department, expand the buying committee early —
our win rate nearly doubles when IT and Security are in the room. Offer a free
architecture review as the vehicle for that introduction.

Finally, we offer a migration credit of up to $25,000 against remaining Competitor X
contract term, capped at six months of their unused spend. This requires VP Sales
approval and proof of their existing contract.

## Traps to Avoid

Never disparage Competitor X by name in writing — it ends up screenshotted in their
Slack. Do not compete on onboarding speed; we lose that comparison. Do not agree to a
feature-by-feature grid without a weighting column, because their 300-integration
marketplace inflates a raw count comparison.
